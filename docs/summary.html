1. About the business

Sushila Phool Bhandar is a flower stall on the road that goes up to Kalkaji Mandir in South Delhi. It is not a shop with a shutter, it is an allotted roadside stall with a tin roof and a blue tarpaulin, sitting between the prasad and chunri sellers.

It was started in 1987 by Ram Kishan Prasad, who sold loose marigold out of a cane basket near the temple gate. He died in 1994 and his wife Sushila Devi carried the work on. Today she runs it with her son Rakesh Kumar, who does the buying at Ghazipur Phool Mandi and handles function orders, and his wife Pooja, who does the threading and the veni work.

Their main product is mogra gajra - jasmine buds hand-threaded on cotton thread, worn in the hair. They also sell bela and chameli gajra, veni, marigold and rose garlands, loose flowers for pooja, and they take decoration orders for weddings, sagai, mundan and griha pravesh. They make between 250 and 400 gajra on a normal day and close to a thousand on Shivratri or the first day of Navratri.

2. Interview notes

Spoken to Rakesh Kumar at the stall, with Sushila Devi joining in the second half. Conversation was in Hindi, notes translated.

Q. Why do you want a website at all?

Rakesh said the phone rings all day with people asking rates, and he is at the mandi from four in the morning so he cannot always pick up. If the rate list is somewhere he can send a link to, that is half his problem gone.

Q. What work actually makes you the most money?

Not the gajra. It is the wedding and function decoration - mandap, car decoration, varmala, gate toran. But people walking past only see a gajra stall and never think to ask. He said this twice, so I made it a whole section on the home page.

Q. Do you want people to order and pay online?

No. Clear no. Flowers change rate every day and he has to see how big the venue is before quoting. He wants the enquiry to land on WhatsApp, where he already does all his business, and then he will call the person.

Q. Is there any problem with people finding you?

Yes. "Near Kalkaji Mandir" is not an address and new customers phone from the metro station asking which side to walk. He wanted directions written properly.

Q. Do you have a logo, or any branding?

No logo. The board on the stall is hand-painted, just the name in Hindi and English. Sushila Devi said not to make anything fancy.

Q. Anything you definitely do not want?

Sushila Devi did not want photos of the family put up, and did not want the site to look "like a big company". Rakesh did not want prices shown as fixed, because the mandi rate moves.

"Rate to roz badalta hai. Likh dena ki phone karke pooch lein."
(The rate changes every day. Write that they should call and ask.) - Rakesh Kumar

3. Website goals

  1. Publish the rate list so it can be sent as a link instead of read out on the phone, with a clear line saying rates move with the mandi.
  2. Show that they do function and wedding work, which is the higher value work nobody knows about.
  3. Get enquiries onto WhatsApp in a usable form - name, phone, date, what is needed - instead of a half-finished phone call.
  4. Help people physically find the stall with real walking directions from the metro.
  5. Look trustworthy and old, because being there since 1987 is genuinely their strongest selling point.

Explicit non-goals, from the interview: no online payment, no cart, no fixed prices, no photos of the family, no login.

Deliberately left out

  • Contact form that sends email - Rakesh does not use email. It would have gone unread. WhatsApp instead, which also means the site needs no server.

6. Design decisions

Colours

Taken off the stall itself, so the site looks like the place it belongs to.

#2f5134

Thread green - the cotton the gajra is strung on. Header, footer, headings.

#e0982a

Genda (marigold). Accent bar, dividers, focus outlines.

#9e2a5c

The pink of the plastic bags they hand flowers out in. Prices, buttons, links.

#faf6ec

Newspaper, which is what flowers get wrapped in. Page background.

#fffdf6

Mogra petal white. Cards and form panels.

Green on cream also stays readable in bright sun, which matters because people will open this standing on the road outside the stall.

Type

Yatra OneHeadings and the shop name. It has the thick, slightly uneven look of a hand-painted Delhi signboard, which is exactly what the stall's real board looks like. Only one weight exists, so it is used sparingly.
MuktaBody text. Chosen because it carries proper Devanagari as well as Latin, so सुशीला फूल भण्डार sits in the same family as the English rather than falling back to a system font.
KalamOnly for the two quotes from Sushila Devi and the caption under the drawing. It is a handwriting face and would be unreadable in bulk.

Layout and branding

  • Single column, one clear thing at a time, maximum width 1020px. Content-first because most of what the site has to give is text - rates, timings, directions.
  • A repeating gajra string drawn in SVG is used as the divider between sections instead of a plain rule. It is the one decorative idea in the whole design and it comes straight from the product.
  • Square corners and flat colour, no shadows or gradients. A roadside stall should not look like a startup.
  • No logo was made. The shop does not have one, and the brief said not to invent one. The name is set in Yatra One and that acts as the wordmark.
  • Hindi appears next to English in the header and in one strip, the way a real shop board is painted in both.

8. Tools used

ToolUsed forWhy this and not something else
HTML5Four static pages The site is four pages of mostly text. Semantic HTML plus a native <details> element handled the FAQ with no JavaScript at all.
CSS3
(Grid + Flexbox)		All layout and styling, one file I started to add Bootstrap and then removed it. It was 200KB to get a grid I could write in six lines, and every Bootstrap site looks like every other Bootstrap site - the opposite of what this shop needed.
Vanilla JavaScriptOpen/closed status, rate search, form validation and the WhatsApp message About 120 lines in one file. No library was needed and jQuery would have been heavier than everything else on the page put together.
Inline SVGThe gajra drawing, the flower divider, the favicon No photographs exist yet and I did not want stock images of somebody else's flowers. SVG stays sharp, weighs almost nothing, and is coloured from the same palette.
Google FontsYatra One, Mukta, Kalam Mukta was the deciding factor because it covers Devanagari properly.
Git + GitHub PagesVersion control and free hosting Free, no server to maintain, and the site is fully static so it fits exactly. This is also why the enquiry form goes to WhatsApp rather than to a backend.

9. Testing

  • Checked on Chrome desktop and on a phone at 375px wide. Also squeezed to 320px, which is where the nav bar was breaking onto two lines until I tightened the padding.
  • All internal links between the four pages checked both ways.
  • Form tested with: nothing filled in, a phone number starting with 1, a two letter message, and a correct entry. It correctly refuses the first three, marks the exact field that is wrong in red, and puts the cursor on it.
  • Phone numbers typed as +91 98111 22333 or 098111 22333 both get cleaned to ten digits before the WhatsApp link is built.
  • Rate search tested with a term that matches nothing, which shows the "nothing matched" message with the phone number in it.

10. What I would do next

  • Photographs. This is the biggest gap - the drawings are a stand-in and real photos of the stall at six in the morning would do more than anything else on this list.
  • A Hindi version of the whole site, not just the header. Half their customers would read it faster.
  • Get them onto Google Business so the stall shows up on Maps, which is probably worth more to them than this website is.
  • A short seasonal note on the home page they could ask me to change - "bela is in, mogra rate is up this week".
