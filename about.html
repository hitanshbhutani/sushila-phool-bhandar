About the shop

Sushila Phool Bhandar is a flower stall on the road going up to Kalkaji Mandir, in South Delhi. It was started in 1987 by Ram Kishan Prasad, who sold marigold and loose flowers out of a cane basket near the gate. After he passed away in 1994 his wife Sushila Devi kept it going, and she still sits at the stall most mornings even though the family keeps telling her not to.

Today it is run by Sushila Devi along with her son Rakesh Kumar and his wife Pooja. Rakesh handles the mandi buying and all the function orders. Pooja does the threading and the veni work, which is the part that takes the longest.

We are a small stall, not a shop with a shutter. Everything is made the same morning it is sold. Whatever is left over at night goes to the mandir for the next day's prasad, because jasmine cannot be kept.

How a gajra is actually made

People ask this a lot, so we are writing it down here.

  • 4:00 am Rakesh leaves for Ghazipur Phool Mandi. Mogra is sold by weight there and the rate changes every single day - in Sawan it can double.
  • 5:15 am Flowers are sorted on a cloth at the stall. Only the closed buds are used. A flower that has already opened will go brown by lunch and nobody wants to wear that.
  • 5:30 am Threading starts. A long needle, ordinary white cotton thread, and a knot after every two flowers so the gajra does not slip. Pooja can do one in about four minutes.
  • 6:30 am Finished gajra go into a wet cloth in a steel container. This is the whole trick - if it dries out the flower opens early.
  • All day Fresh ones keep getting made as the morning lot sells out. On a normal day the last batch is threaded around 4 in the evening.

What is one "haath"? A haath is the length from your fingertips to your elbow, which is how gajra has always been measured here. It comes to somewhere around 45 centimetres. Enough to go once around a normal juda. If you have long hair or want it for a bun on top, ask for dedh haath and we will make it longer.

The years

  • 1987 Ram Kishan Prasad starts selling loose flowers from a basket outside the mandir gate.
  • 1994 He passes away. Sushila Devi takes over the basket, with two children to raise.
  • 2003 The stall gets a proper allotted spot - Shop No. 4 - and a tin roof.
  • 2011 Rakesh joins full time after finishing school and starts taking shaadi orders instead of only counter sale.
  • 2019 We begin daily flower supply to three shops and one office in Kalkaji.
  • 2024 Pooja starts making veni to order, with rose and rajnigandha worked into the mogra.
